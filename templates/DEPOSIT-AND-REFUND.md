# Template: Deposits, Waitlists and Refunds

Taking a deposit before you deliver (or even before you build) is a strong way to test demand and protect your calendar. Tesla has done it with the Roadster since 2017. But how you handle refunds decides whether people trust you next time: some Roadster reservation holders publicly described difficult refunds, and that became part of the story.

**The rule: make it as easy to get your money back as it was to pay, unless you've clearly agreed otherwise up front.**

## Deposit wording (fill in)

```
To lock your [date / slot / place], pay a [amount or %] deposit today.
The rest, [amount], is due [real milestone: on the day / on delivery / in 2 parts].
Refund rule: [one sentence, see below].
```

## Refund rules (pick one, write it in one sentence next to the price)

| Situation | One sentence rule |
| --- | --- |
| Most services | "Fully refundable until [X] days before your date. After that, we keep the deposit to cover the booked time." |
| Pre sale or waitlist | "Fully refundable any time before we start, within 7 days of asking." |
| Custom made products | "Refundable until we start making it. Once we've started, it isn't, because it's made just for you." |
| Events with fixed costs | "50% refundable until [date], then non refundable, because we pay suppliers in advance." |

## Waitlist or pre sale message

```
[Product or level] opens on [real date].
Reserve your place with a [amount] deposit: you'll get [real benefit: first access / the launch price / priority dates].
If you change your mind before [date], you get it all back within 7 days, just reply "refund".
```

## Rules

- Say the refund rule **before** they pay, in one sentence, near the price.
- Put it in writing (the invoice, the booking confirmation).
- Process refunds fast and without arguing when the rule says yes.
- Check local consumer law: some places give customers cancellation rights you can't remove.
- Keep deposits separate in your books until you've delivered.
