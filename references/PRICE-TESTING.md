# Testing New Prices Safely

Pricing is a guess until real customers choose. Test small, measure honestly, and keep the customer's trust.

## What to measure

| Number | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| **Which level people pick** (count per level) | If 80%+ pick Level 1, it's too good or Level 2 is too expensive. If nobody picks Level 3, that's normal; if many do, raise it or limit it more |
| **Conversion** (enquiries that become sales) | Did the new structure help or hurt? |
| **Average sale value** | Total sales ÷ number of customers. Levels usually raise it |
| **Profit per customer** | Price − full cost, averaged |
| **Upgrades from Level 1 to 2** | Is Level 1 doing its job as a way in? |

## 4 safe tests

### 1. New customers only (the safest)
Show the new 3 levels only to new enquiries for 4 weeks. Existing customers keep their current price (or get fair notice). Compare with the 4 weeks before.

### 2. Price the next 10
Quote the new Level 2 price to the next 10 enquiries. If 3 or more say yes, the price is probably not too high. If 0 or 1 do, look at the offer and the wording before dropping the price.

### 3. Add Level 3 only
Keep everything else the same and simply add a premium option. Watch whether your main offer sells more (the anchoring effect) and whether anyone picks the premium.

### 4. Rename only
Keep the same prices but change "Basic, Standard, Premium" to result based names. Watch which level people choose.

## Rules for fair tests

- **Same price for the same thing at the same time,** for everyone in a test. Don't charge two people different prices for the same level on the same day based on who they are.
- **Honour quotes.** If you quoted a price, keep it.
- **Change one thing at a time** where you can.
- **Wait for enough data:** at least 20 to 30 enquiries before deciding.
- **Write the results down** with dates: next time you'll know.
