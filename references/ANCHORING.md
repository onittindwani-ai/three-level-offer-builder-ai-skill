# Why a Premium Level Makes the Middle Look Smart

People judge a price by what's next to it. A 3,599 cleaning alone can feel expensive. Next to a 9,499 "Move in ready" option, it feels sensible. This is called **anchoring**: the first or highest number people see sets their sense of normal.

Tesla's 2026 Roadster line shows it clearly: a 50,000 dollar reservation, a 250,000 dollar reservation, and a third version that is invite only and not approved for public roads. Next to a car most people can't have at all, 250,000 feels like a real option.

## How to use it honestly

1. **The premium level must be real** and worth its price for the people who choose it. Never a fake decoy you'd refuse to deliver.
2. **Show the premium first or alongside.** On a pricing page, put the 3 levels side by side, with Level 2 marked "Recommended". On a call, mention Level 3 first ("Our full done for you option is 9,499..."), then the recommended one.
3. **Make Level 2 the best value.** Anchoring only works if Level 2 really gives the most result per rupee.
4. **Explain the difference in results,** not just price. "Level 3 is the same result, done by the owner, in 48 hours, with a direct line."

## The "decoy" idea (use with care)

Some businesses add an option that exists only to make another one look better: for example a medium coffee priced almost like a large, so the large feels like a deal. This works, but only use it if:
- Every option is a real, honest product people can buy and be happy with.
- The prices reflect real differences.
Never create an option designed to trick people into a choice that's bad for them.

## On a call or in person

```
"Most people go for [Level 2], it gets you [result] in [time] for [price].
If you want it done in 48 hours by me personally, there's [Level 3] at [price], I only take a few of those a month.
And if you'd like to start smaller, [Level 1] at [price] gets you [smaller result], and it comes off [Level 2] if you upgrade within 30 days."
```

Order: recommended, then premium, then the easy yes. The customer hears the hero first, the anchor second, and a safe way in last.
