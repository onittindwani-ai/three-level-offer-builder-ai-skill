# Designing the 3 Levels

## The job of each level

| Level | Its job | Who picks it | How it should feel |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. The easy yes** | Remove the risk of starting with you | New, unsure or small budget customers | "That's a no brainer, let's try" |
| **2. The main offer (the hero)** | Deliver the full result at the best value | Most customers | "This is clearly the right one for me" |
| **3. The honest premium** | Serve the few who want the best, and make Level 2 look smart | Customers who value speed, certainty, status or personal attention | "If I could, this is what I'd have" |

Design in this order: **2, then 1, then 3.** The hero decides everything.

## 8 ways levels can honestly differ

Pick 2 or 3 of these. Don't use all of them.

| Way | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Depth of result** | One part of the problem solved | The whole problem solved | Solved, plus the next problem prevented |
| **Speed** | Standard queue | Priority, a set date | Express, within 48 hours |
| **Who does it** | Self serve, with a guide | Done with you | Done for you, by the owner personally |
| **Support** | Email, 2 working days | WhatsApp, same day | Direct phone line, within 1 hour |
| **Certainty** | No guarantee | A clear guarantee | A stronger guarantee or results review |
| **Duration** | One time | 3 months | 12 months, with check ins |
| **Personalisation** | A standard version | Adjusted to you | Built from scratch for you |
| **Access** | Open to everyone | Open to everyone | By application, invite only or limited per month (only if true) |

## Level 1, the easy yes: rules

- **Small and fast.** A decision in minutes, not days.
- **A real result,** even if small. Never a "teaser" that delivers nothing.
- **Still profitable.** Cover the full cost plus a margin. A small loss is only acceptable as a deliberate, time limited test the owner chose.
- **A natural path to Level 2.** The best Level 1s show the customer exactly why they need Level 2: an audit that finds 5 problems, a first session that builds a plan, a sample pack.
- **Credit option.** Consider: "Your Level 1 fee comes off Level 2 if you upgrade within 30 days." It removes the fear of paying twice.

Good Level 1 shapes: an audit, a diagnosis, a first session, a starter pack, a single item, a trial month, a template or DIY kit, a workshop seat.

## Level 2, the hero: rules

- **The full core result** for a typical customer.
- **What you'd recommend to a friend** with a normal budget.
- **The best value per unit of result.** Compare with Level 1 and 3: Level 2 should give the most result per rupee or dollar.
- **Includes what customers ask for most.**
- **Healthy margin.** This is where most of the profit comes from.
- **Marked as recommended** on the pricing page ("Most popular" only if it's true, otherwise "Recommended").

## Level 3, the honest premium: rules

- **Clearly more:** faster, more personal, more certain, or longer.
- **Limited for a real reason** the owner can say out loud: "I only take 3 of these a month because I do them myself."
- **Priced for the owner's real time and attention.** If Level 3 takes the owner's personal time, price it so that time is worth it, even if only 1 in 10 customers picks it.
- **Exists even if few buy it.** Its job is partly to serve the few and partly to make Level 2 feel reasonable. If nobody ever picks it, that's normal; if many do, raise its price or limit.
- **Never a fake.** Never a level the owner wouldn't actually deliver if someone said yes.

## Common design mistakes

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Levels differ only by quantity (5, 10, 20 pages) | Customers compare numbers and pick the cheapest | Make levels differ by speed, support, certainty or depth |
| Level 1 is too good | Everyone buys Level 1 | Remove one thing most people need, and move it to Level 2 |
| Level 3 is barely different | Nobody sees why to pay more | Add personal attention, speed or a stronger guarantee |
| Too many add ons | Decisions slow down | Bundle add ons into levels; keep at most 2 add ons |
| 4 or 5 levels | Choice overload | Keep 3. Put anything else as "custom, on request" |
| Hiding what's not included | Arguments later | List "Not included" plainly under each level |
| The middle is the cheapest per result but doesn't look like it | People don't notice | Show "Recommended" and the result, not just the features |
